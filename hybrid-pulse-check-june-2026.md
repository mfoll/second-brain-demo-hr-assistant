# Hybrid Pulse Check June 2026

Summary of the short [[pulsewright]] pulse check on hybrid working, run for the [[hybrid-policy-review]] and reported to the [[people-committee-2026-07-15|People Committee on 2026-07-15]].

## Summary

Eight questions sent to the roughly 1,150 hybrid-eligible staff from 2026-06-15 to 2026-06-24. 742 responded (65%). Most supported three office days a week but not the fixed Tuesday-to-Thursday pattern: 58% said the fixed days did not fit their team's work, and 71% said they would prefer one fixed company day with the team choosing the others.

## Key Details

- 63% agreed that three office days a week was reasonable; 22% wanted fewer; 15% more or no view.
- 41% said they had not been able to get a desk on at least one anchor day in May, consistent with the [[head-office-desk-occupancy-2026|Facilities occupancy figures]].
- Most valued office activity: team meetings and onboarding new colleagues; least valued: solo work done at a desk because one is required to be in.
- Reported at division level and for Head Office as a whole; no team cuts, under the 10-respondent rule ([[minimum-reporting-group]]).
- Free-text comments were not collected, by design, to keep it short and low-risk.

## Why It Matters

This is the data behind the partial reversal on 2026-07-15: Tuesday stays fixed, teams choose the other two days ([[hybrid-work-policy-v2]]). My part was the participant file from Talvento and the reminders; [[jun-takahashi]] did the analysis and [[priyanka-desai]] took it to the committee. It also showed that a single-topic pulse check can be run in two weeks, which is behind [[should-pulse-checks-run-quarterly]].

## Caveats

Self-selected response; people unhappy with the fixed days may have been keener to answer. It ran during the desk crunch, not after it. It measures preference, not productivity.
