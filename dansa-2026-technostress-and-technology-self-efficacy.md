# Technostress and exhaustion as threats to work engagement, examining the protective role of technology self-efficacy

Dansa D, Lentor C, Harunavamwe M, Kanengoni H. *Frontiers in Public Health*, 2026.

## Summary

A cross-sectional survey of 202 banking employees, analysed with structural equation modelling. Technostress was associated with more exhaustion and lower work engagement. Technology self-efficacy, people's confidence in using the technology, partly mediated the link to engagement: it supported engagement but did not stop exhaustion under heavy technology demands. The authors suggest building digital skills.

## Why It Matters

Talvento self-service added a new system to every line manager's job in the middle of the year. The finding that confidence with the tool helps engagement but does not cancel the load fits my argument for live, hands-on sessions over e-learning ([[team-leaders-need-floor-training-not-e-learning]]), and for not launching more self-service features during peak.

## Caveats

Bank employees, not warehouse team leaders; cross-sectional.
