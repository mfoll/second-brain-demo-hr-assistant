# Minimum Reporting Group

The smallest number of respondents a survey result may describe before it is shown to anyone. Below it, the result is merged into a larger group, usually the next level up.

## Definition

> [!info]
> At Pellmore the minimum reporting group is 10 respondents for every cut of the 2026 engagement survey and its pulse checks: department, site, division, demographic filter and free-text comments. Set on 2026-06-10 by the Survey Steering Group; 5 in 2024.

## Why it exists

Survey invitations promise that nobody can be identified from their answers. In a group of four or five, colleagues and managers can often guess who said what, especially from comments or with a filter. The rule protects the promise, which protects honest answers. It is a judgement, not a legal number ([[dpo-advice-survey-reporting-threshold]]).

## How it works in practice

- Count respondents, not headcount.
- Apply it after every filter. A department of 40 filtered to "night shift, under one year's service" may be 3.
- Block differencing: two reports that can be subtracted to reveal a small group count as a small group.
- Free-text comments follow the same rule and are screened for names.
- Merge, do not delete: the respondents still count in the site result.

## Related ideas

The idea is close to [[k-anonymity]] for released records, and an application of [[data-minimisation]]: show only what the purpose needs. The same rule applies to my research tables ([[diploma-research-project]]) and to any aggregate in these notes ([[record-hr-cases-without-personal-data]]).
