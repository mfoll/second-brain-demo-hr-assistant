# Structured Interviewing

Asking every candidate for a role the same core questions, in the same order, and scoring each answer against the same anchored scale. The opposite is a free conversation that varies by candidate and by mood. Research summarised in [[job-interview-wikipedia]] finds structured interviews predict job performance better.

## How we use it

- Graduate assessment centres: six fixed competency questions with prompts, two assessors scoring independently before discussing ([[graduate-assessment-centre-pack-2026]]).
- Office and driver vacancies: a scoring sheet per question in Hireloom, recommended in the [[hiring-manager-guide-hireloom]]. Use is patchy; about half of managers fill it in.
- Peak group sessions: a short checklist (shifts, site rules, a practical task) rather than an interview.

## Why I care

Scoring sheets make interviews comparable and give a reason for a decision. That reason is what unsuccessful candidates say they miss ([[candidate-experience-survey-2025]], [[rejection-emails-should-give-a-reason-category]]).

## Limits

Structure reduces some bias but does not remove it; the questions themselves can favour some groups ([[whelpley-2026-selection-tests-and-autistic-applicants]]).
