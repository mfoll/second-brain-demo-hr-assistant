# Payroll Cut-Off Checklist

My first real project at Pellmore: one checklist, agreed with Group Payroll, for what People Services sends before each monthly and weekly payroll cut-off, so changes stop arriving late.

## Outcome

One checklist for monthly and weekly payroll cut-offs agreed with Group Payroll; late changes down from 63 to 17 a month.

## Current State

- Completed on 2025-04-25 ([[payroll-cutoff-checklist-sign-off-2025-04-25]]). Version 2 is in use: [[payroll-cutoff-checklist-v2]]. Version 1 is archived: [[payroll-cutoff-checklist-v1]].
- Late changes have averaged 17 a month since May 2025 ([[payroll-late-changes-log]]). The step-by-step is [[run-monthly-payroll-cutoff]].

## Scope and Boundaries

Everything People Services sends Group Payroll: new starters, leavers, contract and hours changes, manager-approved absence, and one-off payments approved by an HRBP. Not Finance's own journals and not overtime, which comes from the depots' timesheets directly.

## Working Context

When [[deborah-okwuosa]] handed me cut-off coordination in January 2025, there was no list. Each HR assistant sent what they remembered, in emails, to whoever they knew at Group Payroll. [[elspeth-harcourt]] counted 63 late changes in December 2024: changes that arrived after cut-off and either went into the next run or needed a manual off-cycle payment.

I logged every late change for six weeks by cause. The biggest causes were leavers notified after their last day (22), new starter bank details missing (14) and hours changes agreed by managers but never sent (11). The checklist works because it names an owner and a day for each item, rather than because it lists them.

Version 1 (February 2025) tried to cover monthly and weekly in one table. Dariusz said it was unreadable on a Thursday morning. Version 2 splits them and adds the "nothing to send" confirmation, so Group Payroll knows silence means nothing rather than forgotten.

Rejected: a shared mailbox for all payroll changes. Elspeth liked it, but nobody would have owned the mailbox, and it would have hidden who sent what.
