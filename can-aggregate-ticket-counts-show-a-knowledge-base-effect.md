# Can Aggregate Ticket Counts Show A Knowledge Base Effect

The core method question of my [[diploma-research-project]]: with only monthly ticket counts by category, can I say anything about whether the rebuilt knowledge base reduced repeat queries after the hybrid policy changes?

## What the data shows so far

Hybrid tickets were 4% of all tickets before April 2026, 23% in April after v1, and 9% in September after v2 ([[helpdesk-ticket-categories]]). Hybrid articles existed for both changes, but only the v2 set was rewritten from real ticket themes before launch ([[knowledge-base-rebuild]]).

## Why it is hard

- v2 was a smaller change than v1 (one fixed day instead of three, same number of days), so fewer questions would be expected anyway.
- Many things changed at once: manager guidance, the [[people-committee-2026-07-15|July decision]] communications, the end of the desk crunch.
- 25 monthly points and two interruptions five months apart are thin for an [[interrupted-time-series]].
- The handbook says not to overclaim causation from observational data ([[guild-level-5-diploma]]).

## Current Answer

Working answer: the counts can show whether the spike after v2 was smaller and shorter, and whether the [[repeat-query]] share fell. They cannot show why. The report will present the knowledge base as one plausible contributor, with article views as supporting context, and list the alternatives. Harriet agrees with this framing; revisit when the January data is in.
