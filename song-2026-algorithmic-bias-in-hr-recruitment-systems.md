# Algorithmic bias in HR recruitment systems: A qualitative analysis of managerial risk and sociological implications.

Song B. PLoS One, 2026.

## Summary

Proposes a multi-layer framework for finding and reducing bias across the whole life of an algorithmic hiring system: diagnosing the training data, checking fairness at each stage of a multi-stage pipeline, explaining opaque models, and simulating long-term career effects. On synthetic hiring data the framework reduced several standard fairness gaps by roughly a fifth to a third with little loss of accuracy.

## Why It Matters

Read before the DPIA screening meeting on 2026-07-08 ([[ai-screening-dpia-screening-2026-07-08]]). The useful part for us is the starting point: hiring systems trained on past decisions tend to reproduce the patterns in those decisions. That is exactly what Hireloom's "calibration" on our past hires would do ([[hireloom-ai-ranking-module-product-sheet]]). I quoted the idea, not the numbers, in my note to Marek.

## Caveats

Tested on synthetic data only, so the improvement figures say nothing about a real system. Despite the title, I could not see a qualitative study in the abstract. Read from the abstract.
