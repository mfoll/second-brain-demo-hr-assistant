# Psychosocial work experience after implementing hybrid work: a longitudinal study.

Corneliuson C, Chafi MB, Tornevi A, Stjernbrandt A, Wahlström V. *BMC Public Health*, 2026.

## Summary

Compares questionnaire data from 148 white-collar workers in a medium-sized municipal organisation collected in 2017, before the pandemic, and in 2023, after hybrid work had been introduced. Work experience scores improved over time overall and in supportive working conditions, time experience, autonomy and leadership. The main factor linked to the change was sex: women's work experience improved significantly, men's did not.

## Why It Matters

Found during the first week of the [[hybrid-policy-review]] when I was looking for anything on what happens after hybrid starts. It does not compare fixed and flexible days, so it went into my background notes, not the paper. It is a reminder that averages can hide groups whose experience moves differently, which is also why the pulse check was reported by division.

## Caveats

Small sample from one public-sector employer; the two time points are six years apart, so hybrid is not the only change. Read from the abstract.
