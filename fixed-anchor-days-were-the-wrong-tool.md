# Fixed Anchor Days Were the Wrong Tool

## Stance

Three company-wide fixed anchor days were the wrong way to get people together. One shared day plus team-chosen days, which is roughly what we have now, should have been the starting point in April rather than the correction in September. First written on 2026-02-27; updated after the July decision.

## Scope

Head Office and the hybrid-eligible roles at depots and hubs. I have no view on site-based roles beyond [[should-site-based-roles-get-a-flexibility-offer]]. This is my view, not People Services' position, and I did not argue it in meetings.

## Why I Think This

- The consultation said it: 118 comments asked for team choice, 131 warned about desks ([[hybrid-consultation-comment-summary]]).
- The arithmetic was visible in February: 520 desks for 690 people needs attendance spread across the week ([[hot-desking-wikipedia]]).
- The policy assumed a five-day week, and part-time and compressed-hours staff filled the helpdesk in April ([[helpdesk-hybrid-ticket-themes-april-2026]]).
- What managers wanted, according to [[ingrid-salvesen]]'s soundings, was one predictable meeting day, not three.

## What Would Change My Mind

- If Tuesday attendance falls well below the v1 anchor-day level and teams' own days drift apart, the COO's argument that fixed days hold people together gains weight.
- If the quarterly reports show teams' chosen days produce no real overlap.

## Implications

- I should not let this opinion colour the attendance report; the numbers go in as they are.
- For future policy FAQs: draft for part-time and compressed hours from the start.
