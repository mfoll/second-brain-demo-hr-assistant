# Research Project Proposal

My approved proposal for the Unit 7 research project, version 2, approved on 2026-09-22 ([[research-project-scope-approval-2026-09-22]]).

## Summary

Question: does a rebuilt, owned and dated knowledge base reduce the volume and duration of helpdesk queries after an HR policy change? Case: the hybrid work policy at Pellmore, v1 on 2026-04-01 and v2 on 2026-09-01. Data: monthly Deskwell ticket counts by category only.

## Key Details

- Sub-questions: how large and how long was the rise in hybrid tickets after each change; did the rise after v2, when the articles had been rewritten from the April ticket themes, fall faster; what else changed between the two.
- Method: descriptive comparison of monthly shares and a simple segmented trend, framed as an [[interrupted-time-series]] with stated limits. Supporting context: monthly article views in Deskwell, also aggregate.
- Data window: January 2025 to January 2027, 25 months.
- What I will not use: ticket text, ticket-level rows, names, sites below division, any cell under 10.
- Literature: knowledge management and self-service, employee voice, change communication ([[shen-2025-inclusive-climates-and-employee-voice]]).
- Timeline: literature review by 2026-11-30, analysis January 2027, draft to Harriet 2027-02-05, data check by Deborah and Kasia 2027-02-12, submission 2027-02-26.

## Why It Matters

This is the contract for the [[diploma-research-project]]. It replaced v1, which planned to read ticket texts ([[research-project-proposal-v1]]).

## Caveats

Two events, one organisation; it can describe, not prove. The v1 and v2 policies differ in size, which is the biggest threat to the comparison ([[can-aggregate-ticket-counts-show-a-knowledge-base-effect]]).
