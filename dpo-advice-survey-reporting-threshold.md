# DPO Advice On Survey Reporting Threshold

A two-page memo from [[kasia-wrobel]] to the Survey Steering Group, dated 2026-06-03, written ahead of the [[survey-reporting-threshold-decision-2026-06-10|10 June meeting]].

## Summary

Kasia recommends a minimum reporting group of 10 for all scores, comments and filters, plus a block on differencing. Her reasoning is about re-identification, not about the law in the abstract: in small groups the people reading a report usually know the people who answered it.

## Key Details

- Small depots are the main risk: a depot of 18 with 7 respondents and filters by shift or service band can reduce a group to two or three people.
- Free-text comments are the highest risk because writing style and detail identify people even in larger groups. Screen for names and specific incidents before release.
- The survey invitation promises that "no one will be able to identify your answers". That promise should drive the threshold, not the wish to give every manager a report.
- She mentions, as a category only, a 2024 incident in which a manager asked a team who had written a comment. No names, no site.
- Practical points: set the threshold per survey in [[pulsewright]]; keep raw exports on a restricted drive; delete raw responses after 24 months.
- She cites the general idea that a released group should be indistinguishable among at least k members, without proposing a formula ([[k-anonymity]]).

## Why It Matters

The advice the decision followed. It is also the cleanest statement of [[data-minimisation]] applied to reporting that we have, and I reuse its wording in [[check-survey-reports-before-release]].

## Caveats

Advice, not a decision; the Steering Group decided. Kasia said 10 is a judgement, not a legal requirement, and that 8 would be defensible with comments suppressed. That point is in the options for 2027 ([[should-the-survey-reporting-threshold-be-lowered-to-5]]).
