# AI Ranking Is Not Worth It For Warehouse Roles

## Stance

For seasonal and permanent warehouse operative roles, Hireloom's AI ranking would add risk without saving much time. Our real criteria for these roles are already screening questions (shift availability, right to work, willingness to do physical work), and a short group session does the rest. I would not oppose looking at it for roles with genuinely different CVs, such as transport planners.

## Why I Think This

- At the demo, the top factors for an operative role were availability and warehouse experience ([[ai-ranking-vendor-demo-2026-06-18]]). We already filter on the first; the second would push down people with no warehouse history, who make up a lot of good seasonal hires.
- Calibration on our past hires would learn our managers' past preferences ([[song-2026-algorithmic-bias-in-hr-recruitment-systems]], [[algorithmic-bias-wikipedia]]).
- The research mostly measures speed, not whether safeguards work ([[tripathi-2026-llms-in-hiring-scoping-review]]).
- Reading time in peak is real but I suspect smaller than Marek thinks; we have not measured it.

## What Would Change My Mind

Hireloom timestamps showing screening takes more than about 15% of coordinator time in peak; a vendor bias report on a comparable role family showing no meaningful gaps; or a design where ranking only flags incomplete applications.

## Implications

If a DPIA goes ahead, the first test should measure screening time without the tool, so we know what we are trying to save. This differs from [[marek-zielinski]]'s view; I have said so in writing, politely, once.
