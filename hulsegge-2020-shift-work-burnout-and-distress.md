# Shift work, and burnout and distress among 7798 blue-collar workers.

Hulsegge G et al., International Archives of Occupational and Environmental Health, 2020.

## Summary

Compares 4,275 non-shift factory workers with 3,523 rotating five-shift workers, using data from company occupational health checks between 2009 and 2016, with burnout measured on subscales of a Dutch version of the Maslach Burnout Inventory and distress on a symptom questionnaire. Shift work as such was not clearly linked to worse burnout; the association with burnout and distress appeared in workers who were dissatisfied with their shift schedule or felt it had a high impact on their private life.

## Why It Matters

The 2026 survey's workload and wellbeing section was lowest for hub night shifts, at site level ([[engagement-survey-2026-results-summary]]). This paper suggests the question to ask is not "night or day" but "how much say do people have over the rota and how much notice". That matches the most common action in Warehousing plans: rota notice ([[survey-action-plan-tracker-2026]]). Noted for [[sanjay-mehrotra]].

## Key Details

- Large sample, repeated measurements, mixed models adjusted for confounders.
- The finding is about satisfaction with and impact of the schedule, measured by self-report.

## Caveats

Factory workers in one country's companies, data from routine health checks; observational, so it cannot show the schedule causes burnout. Our survey has no burnout scale, only workload items.
