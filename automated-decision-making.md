# Automated Decision-Making

A decision about a person made by a system without meaningful human involvement. In hiring, the obvious case is software that rejects applications below a score without anyone reading them. Data protection law gives people specific protections against decisions like this ([[general-data-protection-regulation-wikipedia]]).

## The line Kasia drew for us

> [!info]
> At Pellmore a person reads every application and makes every hiring decision. A tool may sort or highlight, but nothing may hide or reject an application automatically, and a reviewer who just follows the score is not meaningful human involvement.

That is [[kasia-wrobel]]'s position from the DPIA screening on 2026-07-08 ([[ai-screening-dpia-screening-2026-07-08]]), not my legal reading.

## Why ranking is still a risk

Ranking is not formally a decision, but in a 1,900-application peak a coordinator who reads the top 200 first and fills the roles before reaching the rest has, in practice, let the ranking decide. That is why Hireloom's cut-off setting must stay off and why the DPIA has to look at how reviewers would really use the order ([[hireloom-ai-ranking-module-product-sheet]]).
