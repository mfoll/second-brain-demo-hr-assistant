# Governing algorithms, empowering people: how ethical AI oversight shapes trust, technostress, and employee autonomy in AI-enabled HRM

Patnaik T, Gnankob RI, Nanda NS. *Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence*, 2026.

## Summary

A survey of 290 employees in organisations using AI-enabled HR systems, analysed with structural equation modelling. Transparency, perceived fairness and human-in-the-loop oversight were each associated with more trust in the AI. Transparency was also linked to psychological safety, and human oversight to less technostress. Trust and psychological safety were in turn linked to more employee autonomy, technostress to less.

## Why It Matters

The best argument I have found for why "how it is governed" matters as much as "what it predicts" in HR analytics. If Pellmore ever goes beyond reporting, the conditions it describes (people can see how a result was reached, and a human decides) are the ones [[kasia-wrobel]] would ask for anyway ([[people-analytics-reading-notes]]).

## Caveats

Cross-sectional, self-reported, one country. Associations, not effects.
